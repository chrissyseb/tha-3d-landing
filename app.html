How each mark is explained

How each mark is explained

You don't just get a number. Every criterion in your report comes with the reasons behind its mark.

  1. A mark for each criterionScored out of that criterion's maximum, so you can see exactly where marks were gained and lost.
  2. Evidence from your essayA short quote from your own writing that shows the band reached. A quote is shown only if those exact words appear in your essay.
  3. What this band meansThe band descriptor in plain words, so you know what the mark stands for. Included in detailed feedback.
  4. To reach the next bandWhy the work is not yet in the band above, and the one change that would lift it.

Free instant marking shows the mark, the evidence and the next step for every criterion. Detailed feedback adds what each band means, comments on every paragraph and up to three improvements with rewrites, marked by Exam Preparation Specialists™ and ready within 48 hours.

Scores are estimates against each paper’s published criteria. EssayLabsg™ is not an exam board, and your school’s or examiner’s mark may differ.

Sample results for each paper

Illustrative samples, not real students’ results: an estimated score, what is working and one improvement to try. Each uses a framework our Exam Preparation Specialists™ teach.

PSLEContinuous WritingFree
24/ 36
Mid Band

A clear story with a satisfying ending, but the problem is solved in two sentences and the feelings are told, not shown.

Marked with Story Arc + Show, Don't Tell

Question: Write a composition of at least 150 words about a time you helped someone in need. The pictures are provided to help you think about this topic. Your composition should be based on one or more of these pictures.

Improvement to tryI felt very scared.

Show it: “My palms were clammy and every footstep in the stairwell made me flinch.”

Slow down at the most exciting moment. Give it a full paragraph and let the reader feel it with you.

PSLESituational WritingFree
10/ 14
Upper-Mid Band

All purpose points are covered, but the tone becomes chatty in the closing.

Marked with PACT

Question: Your school canteen throws away a lot of plastic every day. Write an email to your principal proposing a recycling corner in the canteen. In your email, explain where it should be, how it would work and how students can help.

Improvement to tryHope you can say yes ya!

Keep it formal to the end: “I would be grateful if you could consider this proposal.”

Content earns nearly full marks. Tidy the tone and this is a top-band email.

O-LevelEnglish Continuous WritingFree
19/ 30
Mid Band

A vivid opening, but the middle lists sights instead of building one mood.

Marked with Show, Don't Tell

Question: Write 350 to 500 words on the following topic: Describe a place that is at its busiest at night. You should focus on the sights, sounds and atmosphere.

Improvement to tryThere were many stalls selling many things.

Pick one stall and give it a sound, a smell and a person: the hiss of the wok, the uncle shouting orders.

Your first paragraph is already Band 4 writing. Hold that zoomed-in focus all the way through.

O-LevelSituational WritingFree
21/ 30
Task 7/10 · L&O 14/20

Purpose and audience are clear, but two points from the visual text are missing.

Marked with PACT

Question: Your school is planning a learning journey for Secondary 3 students. Study the information about the two venues. Write a report of 250 to 350 words to your principal recommending one venue, giving reasons for your choice.

Improvement to tryIn conclusion, the trip was very fun.

End with a recommendation and a reason drawn from the visual text, e.g. cost per student or travel time.

Tick every bullet in the visual text before you write. That alone can add 2 marks for Task Fulfilment.

O-LevelLiterature in EnglishPlan
12/ 20
Band 3

Good points about imagery, but quotations are retold rather than analysed.

Marked with PEEL-L

Question: How does the poet make the ending of the poem memorable? Support your answer with close reference to the poem.

Improvement to tryThis shows that the speaker is sad.

Zoom in on one word, give its connotation, then its effect on the reader.

You find the right quotes. Now spend two sentences on each one instead of one.

O-LevelSocial Studies SBQPlan
4/ 7
Level 2

A valid inference about the issue, but the cartoonist’s message is not stated or supported.

Marked with LORMS + C-A-P-P

Question: Study Source A. What is the cartoonist’s message? Explain your answer, using details of the source.

Improvement to tryThe source shows that people are unhappy with the policy.

State the message (the cartoonist is critical of…) and prove it with one detail from the cartoon.

Message, then proof, then purpose. Use that order every time.

O-LevelHistory SBCSPlan
5/ 8
Level 3

A sound comparison of content, but reliability is never tested with provenance.

Marked with LORMS + C-A-P-P

Question: Study Sources C and D. How far does Source C prove Source D wrong? Explain your answer.

Improvement to trySource C proves Source D wrong because they say different things.

Check who wrote Source C, when and why, then decide whether its claim can be trusted.

Content gets you to Level 3. Provenance is how you reach Level 4.

O-LevelGeographyPlan
4/ 6
Level 2

Two impacts are named, but neither is developed or linked to a real place.

Marked with LORMS

Question: Explain how tourism can harm coastal environments. [6]

Improvement to tryTourism causes pollution.

Say what pollutes, how it spreads and what it damages, with one named example such as a coral reef.

Point, explain, example. Three developed points beat six one-liners.

A-LevelGP Paper 2 AQFree
6/ 10
Mid Band

Engages with the author’s views, but the Singapore examples are generic.

Marked with R-E-A-L

Question: How far would you agree with the author’s observations? Relate your arguments to your own society. [10]

Improvement to tryIn Singapore, many people also use technology.

Name a specific local policy or trend, then explain why it makes the author’s point stronger or weaker here.

Localise every paragraph. A named Singapore example is worth more than three general ones.

FAQ

Questions from students and parents

Which exams and papers do you mark?

13 papers. Singapore: PSLE English (Continuous Writing and Situational Writing), O-Level English (Continuous Writing and Situational Writing), O-Level Literature in English, Social Studies, History and Geography, and A-Level General Paper (Paper 1 essay and Paper 2 Application Question). International: IB English Language and Literature Papers 1 and 2, and IGCSE First Language English.

What does free instant marking include?

3 free instant markings for PSLE English, O-Level English or A-Level GP. You get an estimated score, band and grade, a mark and short comment for each criterion, and your most important improvement, highlighted on your essay. After you revise, you can re-mark that essay once for free. Free marking doesn’t include paragraph-by-paragraph comments.

What does a detailed feedback report include?

An estimated score and grade, a mark and comment for each criterion, comments on each paragraph, and up to three improvements, each with the sentence to change and a suggested rewrite. It’s marked to criteria and frameworks set by our Exam Preparation Specialists™ and works for all 13 papers. Your reports stay in the Detailed feedback section on the device you used.

How long does it take?

Free instant marking usually takes about a minute. Detailed feedback is marked by Exam Preparation Specialists™ and ready within 48 hours.

How much does detailed feedback cost, and how can I pay?

Monthly plan: S$39 a month by Card, Apple Pay or Google Pay (renews monthly). 31-day pass: S$39 by PayNow, GrabPay, Card or Google Pay (one-off, no renewal). Payment is handled securely by Stripe.

How many reports do I get?

4 detailed reports per billing period: each month on the Monthly plan, or each 31 days on the 31-day pass. Unused reports don’t carry over.

How do renewal and cancellation work?

Monthly plan (Card, Apple Pay or Google Pay): your plan renews monthly until you cancel. Cancel any time with Manage or cancel my plan in the Detailed feedback section, and you keep access for the period you’ve already paid for. 31-day pass (PayNow, GrabPay, Card or Google Pay): a one-off payment with no automatic renewal. If you pay again before your 31 days end, 4 new reports start straight away, the new 31 days run on from the end of your current ones, and unused reports from the earlier payment don’t carry over.

What if a report fails to generate?

If we can't mark your essay, we re-mark it free. If we still can't, we refund that report.

Can I send a photo of a handwritten essay?

Yes, for free instant marking: upload one clear photo of one page, taken flat in good light. Detailed feedback needs typed text of at least 80 words.

Can I use my plan on another phone or laptop?

Your plan and reports are linked to the browser you paid on, and there’s no sign-up. If you change device, message us and we’ll help.

Are the scores official?

No. Scores are estimates against each paper’s published criteria, and your school’s or examiner’s mark may differ. EssayLabsg™ is not an exam board. Every essay is marked the way examiners mark, against your paper’s published criteria, with coaching from the frameworks our Exam Preparation Specialists™ teach.

What happens to my essay?

Your essay is used only to prepare your feedback, including through third-party service providers. Essays sent for free instant marking aren’t kept in our database after marking; your latest result and past results stay in your browser on this device. For detailed feedback, your report, including the question and the lines it quotes from your essay, is saved for up to 400 days so you can reopen it on this device. If you give us your contact details, we keep them for up to 2 years after our last contact, and you can ask us to delete them at any time. To stop our messages, reply STOP or use the unsubscribe link in any email.
